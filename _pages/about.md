My long-term vision is for human beings to have root access to the nervous system — the ability to read its state precisely and to write to it safely, restoring function where it is lost and extending it where it is not.

I am working toward that vision as a data science & innovation engineer at [INOPASE Inc.](https://www.inopase.com/), where I build closed-loop neuromodulation systems that sense nerve activity and stimulate it in response — from clinical study design and neural signal processing to stimulation-control algorithms.

Before that, I trained at [The University of Tokyo](https://www.u-tokyo.ac.jp/en/#) in the lab of [Prof. Kiyoshi Kotani](https://neuron.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/?lang=en), earning my M.Eng (2021) and Ph.D. (2024) in Precision Engineering and continuing there as a research fellow until 2025. My work there spanned both sides of the read/write problem: building mathematical models of neuronal dynamics and gamma oscillations, and running human experiments on transcranial electrical stimulation to modulate visual attention and fluid intelligence. I hold a double B.Eng in Safety Engineering and Biomedical Engineering from [Beihang University](https://ev.buaa.edu.cn) (2019).

My research interests include brain-computer interface, computational neuroscience, nonlinear dynamics and neuromodulation.

I serve as a reviewer for [Cognitive Neurodynamics](https://link.springer.com/journal/11571), [Journal of Computational Neuroscience](https://link.springer.com/journal/10827), [Nonlinear Dynamics](https://link.springer.com/journal/11071), [PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/) and [Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/srep/).

My first name is pronounced "t'ien-i" — any close approximation is fine.
