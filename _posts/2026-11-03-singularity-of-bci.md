---
title: "The Singularity of BCI"
---

In AI, the turning point everyone talks about is AGI: AI that outperforms humans on most tasks. Brain–computer interfaces have no such milestone. We measure progress in electrodes, bit rates and decoding accuracy, but none of these numbers tells us when BCI will actually change how people live.

I think the singularity of BCI is a human decision, not a technical benchmark: **the moment healthy people choose to have a BCI implanted.**

Patients don't mark that threshold. For someone with paralysis or ALS, an implant is a medical choice — the benefit clearly outweighs the risk of surgery, just as with a pacemaker. A healthy person has nothing to restore, only something to gain. For them to say yes, everything has to be right at once: a safe and routine surgery, a durable device, a benefit no phone can offer, private data, and social acceptance. Like "most tasks" for AGI, their willingness is a single signal that sums up all of it.

For non-invasive BCI, the equivalent threshold is the moment people choose to wear one all day, not just for a session in the lab.

And like AGI, this moment is a starting point, not a finish line — the point where BCI moves from repairing the nervous system to extending it.
